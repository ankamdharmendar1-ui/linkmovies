How Foley Artists Use Extinct Plants for Alien Sound Effects

You can't just record an alien screech. You have to invent it, and sometimes that involves a trip to a botanical garden.

Just Now 4 min read By Dharmendar

If you've ever seen a behind-the-scenes video of a Foley artist (the people who make sound effects for movies), you know they use weird stuff. Celery for breaking bones, coconuts for horse hooves—we know the drill. But when they have to create sounds for an entirely alien planet, they have to get seriously weird with it.

The Problem with Normal Sounds

The human ear is incredibly smart. If an alien monster walks on screen and the footsteps sound like someone walking on dry autumn leaves, your brain instantly recognizes the leaves. It breaks the immersion. To fix this, sound designers have started hunting down incredibly rare, and sometimes near-extinct, plants to record audio.

The Botanical Hunt

There are stories of sound designers visiting specialized greenhouses to record the cracking of a specific, rare Madagascan pod, or the weird, squishy tearing sound of a specific tropical fungus. Because 99.9% of the audience has never heard these plants being crushed or snapped, the brain can't place the sound. It just accepts it as 'alien'.

Nature's Synthesis

Instead of just using computers to synthesize digital, fake-sounding noises, combining these bizarre natural sounds gives alien worlds a grounded, organic feel. So the next time you hear a xenomorph hatching or a weird alien fruit being squished on screen, remember that someone probably had to track down a very strange, very real plant to make that happen.
