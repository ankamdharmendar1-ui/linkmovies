Why Are Post-Credit Scenes Suddenly in Every Indian Movie?

We used to leave the theater as soon as the final song ended. Now, directors are forcing us to sit through 10 minutes of scrolling text. Why?

Just Now 5 min read By MoviesFun Expert

For decades, the rule of Indian cinema was simple: the movie ends with a massive dance number or a freeze-frame of the happy family, and you immediately run to the exit to beat the parking lot traffic. But recently, a massive shift has happened. If you walked out early during Vikram, Pathaan, or Salaar, you missed the most important scene in the movie. Welcome to the era of the Indian Post-Credit Scene.

The Marvel Blueprint

We can blame Kevin Feige and the Marvel Cinematic Universe for this. For the last 15 years, Marvel trained global audiences to sit through the credits for a 30-second teaser. Indian directors and producers watched this happen and realized something crucial: Post-credit scenes are the cheapest and most effective marketing tool ever invented.

The Rise of the "Cinematic Universe"

Indian cinema has moved away from standalone sequels and fully embraced the Cinematic Universe. We now have Lokesh Kanagaraj's LCU (Kaithi, Vikram, Leo), Yash Raj Films' Spy Universe (War, Pathaan, Tiger), and Prashanth Neel's interconnected worlds. A post-credit scene is the glue that holds these universes together.

By dropping a massive superstar cameo right at the end of the credits (like Rolex in Vikram or Salman Khan in Pathaan), the director achieves two things:

  • Instant Social Media Virality: The moment the scene drops, Twitter goes into meltdown. It generates weeks of free marketing and insane hype for a movie that doesn't even exist yet.
  • Repeat Viewings: Hardcore fans will literally buy a second ticket just to experience the theater's reaction to the surprise cameo one more time.

The End of the Blooper Reel

In the 2000s, Jackie Chan and some Bollywood comedies used the end credits to show funny bloopers. Today, that real estate is too valuable. The credits are no longer the end of the movie; they are the 30-second trailer for a 500-Crore sequel.
