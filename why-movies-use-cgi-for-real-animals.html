Why Hollywood and Bollywood Now Use CGI for Real Animals (The Hidden Rules)

The tiger in RRR was fake. The dog in Call of the Wild was fake. Here is why the film industry has completely stopped using real animals.

Just Now 5 min read By MoviesFun Expert

If you've watched a major blockbuster recently, you might have noticed something a bit uncanny. Whether it's the dog in Harrison Ford's The Call of the Wild, the horse in John Wick, or literally every single animal in S.S. Rajamouli's RRR, they are all completely computer-generated. Why are studios spending millions of dollars to CGI a normal dog when they could just hire a real one?

The "No Animals Were Harmed" Guarantee

In the past, Hollywood and Bollywood had a very dark history of animal abuse on set to get the perfect shot. Today, organizations like the American Humane Association and the Animal Welfare Board of India (AWBI) strictly monitor film sets. To get the coveted "No Animals Were Harmed" certification, the rules are incredibly strict.

If an action scene involves explosions, loud gunfire, or physical danger, you are legally not allowed to have a real animal on set. It is considered animal cruelty to subject a dog to the deafening sound of a blank-firing machine gun.

The Chaos of Directing a Cat

Aside from the legal and ethical reasons, there is a massive practical reason: animals are terrible actors. A movie set costs hundreds of thousands of dollars an hour to run. If a scene requires a dog to look at the villain and bark exactly on cue, a real dog might take 50 takes to get it right. A CGI dog does it perfectly on the first try, saving the studio time and money.

The RRR Standard

In India, the AWBI rules are so notoriously strict that S.S. Rajamouli decided to completely bypass them for RRR. Rather than dealing with the massive red tape and ethical concerns of bringing a real tiger or wolf onto a set, he spent a massive chunk of his VFX budget creating hyper-realistic digital animals. He even put a disclaimer at the beginning of the movie proudly stating that 100% of the animals were digital.

So, while CGI animals might occasionally look a little bit like a video game, it is a necessary evolution. It keeps the animals safe, keeps the budgets in check, and lets the directors create impossible action scenes.
